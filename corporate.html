MARKETING

Context of Campaign

About us

130dB is more than just another brand; we are a powerful movement. We want to amplify the opinions of those who live life loud, fierce and free, giving them a space where they can truly express themselves. At 130dB, we believe that music is more than just music; it’s something that brings people together to celebrate authenticity, and rebellion and living life with high energy.

Our mission is to give the new generation of rock lovers who are craving authentic (new) experience, a space where they can be themselves in the raw, unjudged energy of hard rock. With our bold visuals, outstanding messages, and interactive content on Instagram, we aim to inspire a sense of freedom and power with every post, inspiring people. We are here to break the norms and provide a place for those who want to truly be themselves.

Our concert venue in Breda is more than just another concert venue, it’s a place to discover new underground hard-rock talent and experience the thrill of live performances in a setting designed for true hard-rock lovers. Whether people are there to discover the next big band or to just enjoy music and dance freely, 130dB is the place where passion for rock music is celebrated loudly.

As 130dB, we are proud to be a space for the bold, fearless and unapologetic rock fans of today and tomorrow. We amplify voices that need to be heard, as we continue to grow, push boundaries, and share the love for rock music, as together we keep it alive.

Learning Points

Context of our campaign

With this marketing campaign, we want to reach our core audience: hard rock lovers aged 18-28 who LIVE for music and are constantly looking for fresh, new, raw talent. These are the people who crave the adrenaline of a live performance. And where will we find those people? On Instagram. That is where 130dB went for their marketing strategies.

We created content that’s LOUD, BOLD, and 100% in line with our brand identity. Think about high-energy visuals, inspiring quotes, relatable memes, funny reels and impressions of our venue in Breda. The content is designed to speak their language and make them feel seen. Our goal? To get a follower community around the spirit of 130dB, people who all share the same passion as we do.

We want our Instagram account to feel like an extension of our website. That means that we have the same visual vibe, with black and pink as our main colours, our logo on most posts, and a consistent look that screams 130 dB. When it comes to how we speak to our audience, we keep it BOLD, DARING and INFORMAL. We are on Instagram to shout our vision and passion out loud.

We use caps in our captions to give it an extra dimension and attractive to read. We use a lot of emojis to catch the eye. Our tone is always positive and empowering because we’re not just building a fanbase; we’re trying to build a movement.

To boost our reach, we use targeted hashtags like #hardrock #concerts #rock #headbanging #music, to target people that watch similar content.

Future Plans

Marketing Objectives (SMART)

REACH

1. To get seen:

Our goal was to hit an average of 600 views per post by April 15th by consistently posting engaging and optimised content, to increase overall campaign exposure and reach more eyes on us.

2. To grow our fanbase:

We aimed to boost our follower count by 100 by the 15th of April through a mix of bold visuals, powerful messages, and diverse content.

AFFECT

3. Expressive comments:

By April 15th, we set a goal to have an average of 10 comments under our posts being expressive (such as emojis or phrases showing excitement for hard rock, like 🔥 or 🤘🏽) by posting content with catchy captions, deep topics and community-centred posts to get people to comment.

4. Likes and engagement:

For our Instagram posts, we wanted to have an engagement with an average of at least 20 likes. We used humour and catchy captions to make our followers feel important, seen and heard.

RESPONSE

5. Fan conversations:

We wanted to create real engagement by encouraging our followers/viewers to share their personal music tastes. Our goal was to ask questions like “What’s your favourite band?” in specific posts and get at least 5 people to comment their answer under each one.

Campaign Evaluation

The campaign had a slower start than expected. Our first few posts received less interaction than we anticipated, and our audience engagement was limited in the first two weeks. It felt a bit disappointing at the start, especially considering the effort we put into planning the content, but it also gave us motivation to keep going to reach our objectives and to see what is possible.

However, when we continued the campaign by experimenting with posting more different types of content, we started to have a better understanding of what the audience likes to see and what they respond to. We started to post reels (and memes) with humour; this is where we started to see stronger results as it resonated more with our target audience than static posts or standard announcements.

Once we started posting more content that’s more engaging and entertaining, we saw that more people interact with it. Our reels got more views than we expected, and we got a lot more comments than we got with the static posts or memes. The follower count on our Instagram account increased in weeks 3 and 4, and the overall reach of our posts improved a lot.

By the end of the campaign, we felt like we had built our own 130dB community with like-minded people. The audience is slowly starting to connect with the tone, vibe and personality of our brand, which is exactly what we are aiming for.

Professionalism

Evaluation of Reaching Our Objectives

1. Reaching our view goal (600 views per post)

We set a goal to have an average of 600 views per post by April 15th. Over the 15 posts that we published during the campaign, we saw mixed results in terms of views. The first posts (static images like the logo and brand introduction) performed below expectations, with an average of between 97 and 205 views. These contributed to a slow start. However, more varied content like memes, short-form reels, and humorous posts showed better results.

  • Post 7 (name explanation): 549 views
  • Post 9 (POV: dino meme): 516 views
  • Post 14 (funny reel): 842 views

These posts were key in shifting audience interaction. Reels and humour-driven posts clearly performed better. Although we didn’t hit our target average of 600 views, we saw steady growth, and one post even surpassed the goal, proving the strength of dynamic and relatable content.

2. Follower growth (Goal: 100 followers)

The campaign started with just a few followers, but by the end of week two, we had grown to 69 followers. Engagement picked up after introducing more relatable, high-energy content. By April 15th, we reached 117 followers, surpassing our goal.

3. Expressive comments (Goal: 5 per post)

We aimed to get at least 5 expressive comments (emojis or reactions) per post. Here's a breakdown:

  • Posts 1–3 (logo): 0 comments
  • Post 4: 2 emoji-only comments
  • Post 5: 1 emoji comment
  • Post 6 (Dino meme): 0 comments
  • Post 7 (venue impressions): 0 comments
  • Post 8 (meme): 0 comments
  • Post 9 (reel): 1 comment (text + emoji)
  • Posts 10–11 (static): 0 comments
  • Post 14 (humorous reel): 12 comments (6 emojis, 6 with personal band names)
  • Final post: 0 comments

Only Post 14 reached and exceeded the goal. Humour and relatability clearly drive conversation. Static visuals and logo posts didn’t inspire much interaction.

4. Likes per post (Goal: 20 average likes)

Our early posts received 2–10 likes. Static quotes and announcements averaged 5–12 likes. However, reels and more engaging content saw better numbers:

  • First reel: 27 likes
  • Funny reel: 33 likes
  • Intro post: 19 likes
  • Name explanation: 16 likes

While our overall average (12 likes) was under target, reels performed the best, again showing humour and dynamic formats drive more interaction.

5. Real conversations (Goal: 3+ answers to caption questions)

We added conversation starters in multiple captions but saw mixed responses:

  • Name explanation post (“comment your favourite emoji”) = 0 comments
  • Quote post (“tag that friend who is obsessed with rock.”) = 0 comments
  • Dino meme post (“tag someone to win tickets.”) = 0 comments
  • Reel: venue impression (“comment your favourite rock emoji.”) = 2 comments
  • Reel: “What’s your favourite rock artist?” = 5 comments

Only one post met the 5-comment goal. Question captions alone weren’t enough—pairing them with a strong reel made the difference.

Key Learnings

Weeks 1–4 Overview

In weeks 1 and 2, we launched the campaign with static posts to establish our visual identity and introduce our brand. Unfortunately, these posts received low engagement, both with likes and comments, which gave us a slow start and made us slightly discouraged in the beginning. In weeks 3 and 4, we began to experiment with new types of content like memes and reels. This change brought improvement in both views and interaction, and we started to better understand what kind of content resonated with the audience that we wanted to reach.

What Worked Well

Reels and humour-driven content performed better than static visuals. Posts like the funny reel (842 views, 33 likes, 12 comments) proved that short, relatable videos can drive real engagement. Follower growth was consistent in the last two weeks of the campaign, especially as we introduced more personality and less polished, more human content.

What Didn’t Work

Static content like logo posts, quotes, and general announcements consistently underperformed. These are posts that you want to have on your account to inform, but people do not interact with them at all. Posts with a conversation-starter almost didn’t encourage interaction, even when we included specific Call to Actions like “comment your emoji” or “tag a friend,” most of those posts received little to no response.

We expected the venue-focused reels to create hype, but these were overlooked by our followers, and they didn’t interact with them at all. What we didn’t expect was that the memes didn’t create interactions. We really liked those with funny humour in there, and with other accounts it often works, but for us it didn’t.

What Went As Expected

Reels brought more reach, especially once we leaned into humour with trending sounds. Our brand tone (bold, energetic, hard rock-inspired) found more connection with our audience in the second half of the campaign, because then we already had a little community of followers, which made the account more attractive to new followers.

One of the most important lessons we learned from this campaign is that content format matters. Static posts, like quotes or logo visuals, were easy for people to scroll past, while reels and funny or relatable content made them stop and engage. We noticed that reels are especially powerful for growing reach and interaction, especially when they use trending audio or jokes that people recognise. Another key takeaway is that just asking questions in captions isn’t enough to get engagement from people. If we want comments, we need to trigger a real reaction, whether that’s laughter, nostalgia, or personal connection.

What We Would Do Differently Next Time

Launch with more personality-forward content earlier in the campaign. We should also focus more on short-form reels right from the start. We should use interactive features like polls, sliders, and quizzes in our stories to start conversations more easily, and we should encourage comments with stronger incentives (e.g. giveaways, shoutouts) and follow up with replies to keep conversations going.

Conclusion

This campaign was a valuable learning curve. While we didn’t meet every objective, we gained a much clearer understanding of what our audience wants and how to connect with them. We now know that the energy, humour, and community aspect of our brand must be front and centre, especially through Reels and interactive content. This gives us a solid foundation to build stronger campaigns going forward.

Future Planning

If we were to continue this campaign or launch a new one, here are the main things we would change based on what we’ve learned:

  • Start with reels and humour-based content right away instead of static intro posts. These formats perform better and create immediate engagement.
  • Use trending audio and more creative hooks in reels to improve visibility and relatability.
  • Reduce the number of static posts or give them a stronger visual trigger and message to keep people interested.
  • Improve our Call to Actions by making them more unique, interactive, emotional, or funny, because just asking questions in the caption doesn’t always work.
  • Use Instagram Stories more actively with polls, questions, and behind-the-scenes content to build daily engagement!
  • Encourage comments: with, for example, shoutouts, giveaways, or direct replies to people who engage.
  • Plan content with weekly themes or series, so the account feels more consistent and so that followers know what to expect from the account and therefore want to follow, so that they don’t miss anything.

Conclusion

For the future, we now have a much better idea of what works and what doesn’t. By focusing more on reels, relatability, and real community interaction, we can build a stronger and more engaged community for our brand 130dB, one that truly connects with the vibe of our brand.
