Production Justification

User Interface Patterns and Website Design Justification for 130dB

Target Audience and Brand Identity

130dB is more than just a venue — it’s a movement for underground hard rock lovers who crave community, authenticity, and visceral music experiences. The visual design and interface patterns reflect this identity by using raw, high-contrast visuals, energetic layouts, and interactive elements that keep users immersed in the scene. Every design choice is deliberate — crafted to engage real fans, build loyalty, and reflect the power of live music.

User Interface Patterns

Hero Section (Homepage)

The homepage hero makes an immediate statement with a bold image and high-contrast text featuring the brand’s name and vibe. It doesn’t just grab attention — it sets the tone for the entire website. The dark color palette and bold magenta touches reflect both rebellion and underground culture. The tagline instantly informs users what 130dB stands for.

Display Cards

Display cards are used across the site to group information in visually digestible blocks — used under “Why Us”, “Reviews” on the Home page, and in the “Meet the Team” section on the About page. These cards let users quickly process multiple bits of content without overwhelming them, supporting both fast browsing and mobile-friendly readability.

Image Carousel (Homepage: Upcoming Events)

A horizontal carousel is used to showcase upcoming events. This isn’t just a flashy gimmick — it’s a UX-centered decision. It allows fans to flip through upcoming gigs without feeling overwhelmed or having to scroll endlessly. It invites interaction and keeps engagement high.

Text Carousel (Venue Page)

Used in the “Values” section to highlight one core belief at a time, this design element emphasizes focus and emotion. Instead of dumping text, the user is guided through 130dB’s values slowly, one by one — just like how underground movements are built: intentionally and with depth.

Purchase Path

Though not active for ticket sales yet, these interface patterns simulate a fully-functional ticketing system — giving users the familiarity of an e-commerce experience. The goal is to keep the journey intuitive and authentic to modern fans who expect seamless transactions.

Endless Spinner

Activating the endless spinner on “Place Order” was an intentional mock-up decision — acting as a placeholder for a future payment integration while showing understanding of user flow and expectation. It reflects knowledge of typical checkout experiences, adding credibility to the UX flow.

Fake Contact Form

Even as a placeholder, this form design simulates a professional brand willing to hear from its audience. It supports the narrative of fan connection, accessibility, and professionalism, even if the backend is still under development.

Two-Column Layout

This layout is used to display event details, artist bios, or company backstory in a structured, scannable format. It creates balance between visuals and text, optimizing screen space while supporting clear storytelling — a solid UX pattern for both desktop and mobile.

Navigation Bar

The fixed-top navbar gives users constant access to key pages like “Upcoming Events”, “About Us”, and “Contact.” Including icons like the cart creates familiarity, while ensuring that fans can jump to any section at any time — which is essential in a high-energy, information-heavy website.

Footer with Social Links

Every page (excluding Corporate) ends with a clean footer containing social media links (Instagram, TikTok), which allows fans to connect on platforms they already use. This supports a cross-channel content strategy and increases brand touchpoints beyond the website.

How Each Page Reflects UX, Strategy and Brand Purpose

Home

The homepage sets the tone with energy and boldness. It grabs attention with visuals and hierarchy and call to actions wiht the buttons right at the start, then funnels users toward core content — upcoming shows, what makes 130dB different, and what real fans are saying.

UX Strategy in Action:

  • Bold hero to make a first impression
  • Carousel to reduce overload and keep fans engaged
  • Use of parallax give the homepage very stong and unique experience

About Us

Rather than overwhelm with info, this page creates an emotional story. It tells the “why” behind the brand, and introduces the team using friendly cards and text carousel. The layout is scannable and human, making the brand feel real and trustworthy.

UX Strategy in Action:

  • Focus on storytelling, not jargon
  • Visual hierarchy and split layout improve readability
  • Values carousel isolates each message for better absorption

Event

This section uses structured event listings in a two-column layout, featuring images, dates, and descriptions. Easy to scan, aesthetically tight, and organized for action — like buying tickets or getting hype for a gig.

UX Strategy in Action:

  • Bigger name of the name as you click on it, dragging attention and bringing excitement
  • Consistent structure across listings
  • Gallery boost memory recall of events or excitement to see in real life
  • CTA-style buttons make it clear what to do next
  • Purchase possible staright on Ticketmaster

Contact Page

Simplified and friction-free, this page is built to feel accessible. Users aren’t buried in contact options — one form, one goal. The design communicates care and openness, and matches user expectations.

UX Strategy in Action:

  • Image Carousel - engaging to see and scroll the photos
  • Simple form → less user anxiety
  • Clear structure → easier navigation
  • Reinforces emotional connection post-purchase or inquiry

Design, Theme and Consistency

  • Color: The magenta and black theme screams underground rock. It reflects energy, rebellion, and intensity — resonating with both younger fans and die-hard music lovers.
  • Fonts: Sharp, all-caps fonts match the sound of a live riff — bold and in your face.
  • Imagery: Performance shots and urban-style edits show that 130dB isn’t polished pop — it’s raw, live, and loud.

Final Summary

The 130dB website is more than a promotional space — it’s a digital extension of a live rock experience. With a focus on immersive interaction (carousel, parallax, animated elements), emotional storytelling, and clean UX design, the site builds trust, attracts fans, and reflects the rebellious, community-driven heart of underground music.

It balances performance and attitude — just like the genre itself.

Testing

Testing Overview

To evaluate the effectiveness of the website in fulfilling its communication goals, promoting hard rock events, facilitating ticket purchases, allowing venue bookings, encouraging newsletter sign-ups, and increasing user engagement, we conducted usability testing sessions. The primary goal was to assess whether the website successfully communicates its purpose and enables users to complete key tasks with ease.

Participants

Four participants were selected to closely match the target audience of the website: individuals with an interest in hard rock music, frequent eventgoers, and users comfortable with digital platforms.

Participant Age Gender Background Relevance
P1 (Mike) 21 Male Guitarist in a local student band, regular concertgoer Directly aligns with hard rock audience
P2 (Fleur) 19 Female Studies event management, listens to rock Represents younger target demographic
P3 (Lado) 26 Male University student, buys tickets for gigs monthly Interested in music events
P4 (Adrian) 21 Male Music blogger, regular concertgoer Strong online user; interest in events

Testing Environment

Testing was conducted in-person. Each session lasted approximately 15 minutes. Participants were asked to think aloud while navigating the site. We provided minimal guidance to allow for natural user interaction.

Testing Goals

  • Evaluate if the website clearly communicates its focus on hard rock events.
  • Test the usability of key features:
    • Browsing event listings
    • Purchasing tickets
    • Sending inquiries
    • Booking the venue
    • Signing up for the newsletter
    • Navigating to the "About Us" page
  • Identify usability issues that may prevent users from completing tasks or understanding the site’s purpose.

Tasks Assigned to Participants

  • Find and explore a hard rock event happening next month.
  • Purchase a ticket for the event you chose (go as far as possible).
  • Send an inquiry to the company asking a question about something.
  • Book the venue for a private event next month.
  • Find the 'About Us' page and describe what the company is about.
  • Sign up for the newsletter.
  • Describe what you think the website is for after using it.
  • Give one thing you’d improve and one thing you liked.

Testing Findings

1. Clarity of Purpose

  • Most participants (3 out of 4) immediately recognized the hard rock theme from visuals, fonts, and imagery.
  • P2 suggested adding a homepage tagline clarifying the venue’s hard rock focus.

2. Event Listings

  • All users successfully found upcoming events.
  • P3 suggested adding a date filter.
  • Fix: Add a filter bar with date range above event listings.

3. Ticket Purchase

  • All users completed ticket selection without issues.

4. Sending an Inquiry

  • All users found the inquiry form.
  • 3 out of 4 noted no confirmation message after submission.
  • Fix: Add a success pop-up: “Your message has been sent - we’ll get back to you soon!”

5. Booking the Venue

  • All participants found the booking page informative and clear.
  • Form had a bug with the date/time picker that blocked submission.
  • Fix: Resolve date/time picker bug and confirm successful submission visually.

About Us Page

  • All users found it easily and liked the tone.
  • P3 suggested adding photos or videos from past events for credibility.
  • Fix: Add visuals from real events to the About section.

Newsletter Sign-Up

  • All users signed up successfully.
  • P4 and P2 said there was no info on what the newsletter includes.
  • Fix: Add text like “Monthly updates on upcoming hard rock events and exclusive offers.”

Summary of Actionable Findings

Conclusion

Overall, users found the experience engaging and easy to navigate, but several usability improvements were identified that will enhance clarity and engagement. Implementing the recommended changes will ensure the site more effectively achieves its goals and provides a better user experience.

MARKETING

Marketing and Communication Plan

Target Group

Our primary audience includes hard rock music fans aged 18–28 living in or close to Breda. Many are students or early-career professionals with a moderate income. They are authentic, rebellious, and want to be unapologetically themselves, often identifying with underground subcultures. They seek energetic (live) music and discover new underground (hard) rock bands. They value live music over mainstream productions.

This audience frequently engages with music on platforms like Instagram, Spotify, and YouTube to watch back live performances. Our target group often uses these socials on their phones, and they prefer short-form content, as Gen Z has a lower attention span. Often, they are frustrated by the lack of hard rock events, high ticket prices, and difficulty in discovering new good bands. Also, they would like to change the stereotype around hard rock fans, as they feel misunderstood.

Media Tactics

Platform

We are using Instagram as our platform to market our brand. We are creating interactive posts and reels, so Instagram is the correct platform to do this on. We make use of feed posts, stories, and reels to maximize engagement.

Content Type

We are creating Reels (short, engaging videos that are designed to hopefully go viral), Stories (to get more engagement with our account and get people to be reminded more of our Instagram account by popping up in their stories feed), and Feed Posts/Carousels (with high-quality concert photos, announcements, fan moments, and impressions of the venue).

Campaign Evaluation

The campaign had a slower start than expected. Our first few posts received less interaction than we anticipated, and our audience engagement was limited in the first two weeks. It felt a bit disappointing at the start, especially considering the effort we put into planning the content, but it also gave us motivation to keep going to reach our objectives and to see what is possible.

However, when we continued the campaign by experimenting with posting more different types of content, we started to have a better understanding of what the audience likes to see and what they respond to. We started to post reels (and memes) with humour; this is where we started to see stronger results as it resonated more with our target audience than static posts or standard announcements.

Once we started posting more content that’s more engaging and entertaining, we saw that more people interact with it. Our reels got more views than we expected, and we got a lot more comments than we got with the static posts or memes. The follower count on our Instagram account increased in weeks 3 and 4, and the overall reach of our posts improved a lot.

By the end of the campaign, we felt like we had built our own 130dB community with like-minded people. The audience is slowly starting to connect with the tone, vibe and personality of our brand, which is exactly what we are aiming for.

Key Learnings

Weeks 1–4 Overview

In weeks 1 and 2, we launched the campaign with static posts to establish our visual identity and introduce our brand. Unfortunately, these posts received low engagement, both with likes and comments, which gave us a slow start and made us slightly discouraged in the beginning. In weeks 3 and 4, we began to experiment with new types of content like memes and reels. This change brought improvement in both views and interaction, and we started to better understand what kind of content resonated with the audience that we wanted to reach.

What Worked Well

Reels and humour-driven content performed better than static visuals. Posts like the funny reel (842 views, 33 likes, 12 comments) proved that short, relatable videos can drive real engagement. Follower growth was consistent in the last two weeks of the campaign, especially as we introduced more personality and less polished, more human content.

What Didn’t Work

Static content like logo posts, quotes, and general announcements consistently underperformed. These are posts that you want to have on your account to inform, but people do not interact with them at all. Posts with a conversation-starter almost didn’t encourage interaction, even when we included specific Call to Actions like “comment your emoji” or “tag a friend,” most of those posts received little to no response.

We expected the venue-focused reels to create hype, but these were overlooked by our followers, and they didn’t interact with them at all. What we didn’t expect was that the memes didn’t create interactions. We really liked those with funny humour in there, and with other accounts it often works, but for us it didn’t.

What Went As Expected

Reels brought more reach, especially once we leaned into humour with trending sounds. Our brand tone (bold, energetic, hard rock-inspired) found more connection with our audience in the second half of the campaign, because then we already had a little community of followers, which made the account more attractive to new followers.

One of the most important lessons we learned from this campaign is that content format matters. Static posts, like quotes or logo visuals, were easy for people to scroll past, while reels and funny or relatable content made them stop and engage. We noticed that reels are especially powerful for growing reach and interaction, especially when they use trending audio or jokes that people recognise. Another key takeaway is that just asking questions in captions isn’t enough to get engagement from people. If we want comments, we need to trigger a real reaction, whether that’s laughter, nostalgia, or personal connection.

What We Would Do Differently Next Time

Launch with more personality-forward content earlier in the campaign. We should also focus more on short-form reels right from the start. We should use interactive features like polls, sliders, and quizzes in our stories to start conversations more easily, and we should encourage comments with stronger incentives (e.g. giveaways, shoutouts) and follow up with replies to keep conversations going.

Conclusion

This campaign was a valuable learning curve. While we didn’t meet every objective, we gained a much clearer understanding of what our audience wants and how to connect with them. We now know that the energy, humour, and community aspect of our brand must be front and centre, especially through Reels and interactive content. This gives us a solid foundation to build stronger campaigns going forward.

Future Planning

If we were to continue this campaign or launch a new one, here are the main things we would change based on what we’ve learned:

  • Start with reels and humour-based content right away instead of static intro posts. These formats perform better and create immediate engagement.
  • Use trending audio and more creative hooks in reels to improve visibility and relatability.
  • Reduce the number of static posts or give them a stronger visual trigger and message to keep people interested.
  • Improve our Call to Actions by making them more unique, interactive, emotional, or funny, because just asking questions in the caption doesn’t always work.
  • Use Instagram Stories more actively with polls, questions, and behind-the-scenes content to build daily engagement!
  • Encourage comments: with, for example, shoutouts, giveaways, or direct replies to people who engage.
  • Plan content with weekly themes or series, so the account feels more consistent and so that followers know what to expect from the account and therefore want to follow, so that they don’t miss anything.

Conclusion

For the future, we now have a much better idea of what works and what doesn’t. By focusing more on reels, relatability, and real community interaction, we can build a stronger and more engaged community for our brand 130dB, one that truly connects with the vibe of our brand.

Lean Canvas

Phase 1

1. Problem and Solution

Problem 1: The cost of attending live music events is a barrier
Fans of hard rock often face high ticket prices, limiting their ability to attend live shows regularly. This financial barrier discourages engagement with the local music scene.

Solution: Keep prices low
130DB prioritizes affordability by minimizing overhead costs (e.g., partnering with underground artists, optimizing venue operations) to offer tickets at accessible prices.

Problem 2: Desire for more local/underground hard rock shows
Existing venues rarely cater to hard rock, leaving fans with few opportunities to experience live performances of their preferred genre.

Solution: A venue focused only on hard rock
130DB fills this gap by dedicating its space exclusively to hard rock, creating a hub for fans and artists alike.

Problem 3: Lack of dedicated platforms for hard rock culture
The absence of a centralized space for hard rock culture has fragmented the community, making it harder for fans to connect and discover new music.

Solution: Geo-targeted event recommendations
130DB uses location-based marketing (e.g., social media ads, targeted notifications) to ensure fans never miss nearby events, creating a stronger local scene.

2. Customer Segments

  • Young rock/metal fans: young fans (16–24) who are passionate about hard rock and eager for affordable live experiences.
  • Local underground bands/artists: Emerging musicians seeking exposure and a dedicated platform to perform.

3. Unique Value Proposition

A pure hard rock venue where fans discover new bands and feel the scene come alive again.

130DB stands out by:

  • Exclusivity: The only venue in town dedicated to hard rock.
  • Discovery: A stage for underground talent, helping fans find new bands.
  • Community: Reigniting the energy and unity of the classic hard rock scene.

4. Unfair Advantage

  • No other venue does this in town: 130DB has zero local competition as the sole hard rock-focused venue.
  • Direct relationships with underground artists: Partnerships with bands ensure a steady lineup of authentic, high-energy performances.
  • We love rock and know the scene: The team’s deep genre expertise and passion translate into curated events that resonate with fans.

5. Channels

  • Website: Central hub for event schedules, tickets, and artist spotlights.
  • Instagram and TikTok: Viral promotion through short clips, behind-the-scenes content, and collaborations. These platforms align with the target audience’s digital habits and maximize reach.

6. Key Metrics

  • User feedback on price satisfaction (via post-event surveys): Measures affordability success.
  • Engagement with geo-targeted notifications: Tracks effectiveness of localized marketing.
  • Event attendance: Indicates overall demand and venue popularity.

7. Revenue Streams

  • Ticket sales: Primary income from fans attending events.
  • Bar and snacks: Secondary revenue from beverages during shows.

8. Cost Structure

  • Fixed costs: Rent, staff salaries, licenses, insurance, utilities.
  • Variable costs: Artist booking fees, event production, security, promotion (posters, paid ads).

5. Channels

At 130dB, our goal isn’t just to promote events, it’s to build a lasting relationship with fans by meeting them where they are, delivering real value, and creating excitement well before the first guitar riff hits the stage. Based on direct fan interviews and digital behavior insights, our most effective and scalable marketing channels are Instagram and Email Newsletters, with additional opportunities in physical presence and local partnerships.

Instagram – Top Channel

Interviewees said Instagram is their #1 source for events, we’ll post weekly band teasers.

Why it works:

According to our interviews, a massive 75% of fans cited Instagram as their primary source for discovering new events. It's the go-to platform for our demographic - visually driven, fast-moving, and perfectly tailored to the aesthetic and energy of the underground rock scene.

Strategy:

  • Weekly Band Teasers: Every week, we’ll spotlight upcoming performers with high-quality visuals, mini-interviews, and behind-the-scenes clips to generate hype and strengthen artist-fan connection.
  • Instagram Stories and Reels: Quick, raw content, soundchecks, backstage sneak peeks, venue setup, fan shout-outs, will help humanize the venue and turn passive followers into active participants.
  • Countdowns + Polls: Tools like Instagram’s countdown stickers and polls will be used to drive urgency and foster interaction.
  • Tag-for-Entry Giveaways: Ticket giveaways via “tag a friend” campaigns will help organically expand reach, encouraging new fan discovery through existing networks.
  • Branded Aesthetic: Our visuals will maintain a high-voltage rock aesthetic that mirrors the energy of the venue, which helps build a recognizable and ownable brand presence.

Why it’s defensible:

We’re not just posting to post, we’re telling stories, building a vibe, and creating anticipation. Our consistent tone, fan-centered approach, and artist-first content make our page more than just an event listing - it becomes part of the scene.

Email Newsletters – Direct-to-Fan Engagement

Why it works:

In our interviews, 100% of fans said they’d sign up for updates. That’s rare. This shows a high level of investment in staying connected and informed, especially from a loyal, niche community. Email gives us a direct line to fans without being at the mercy of social media algorithms.

Strategy:

  • Weekly "This Week at 130dB" Newsletter: A clean, bold format that includes upcoming shows, featured bands, exclusive content, and loyalty point reminders.
  • Segmented Audiences: Fans can select preferences (e.g., only want “New Blood Nights” or want VIP alerts), ensuring content feels personalized.
  • Early Access + Presales: Subscribers get early access to popular gigs or exclusive invites to secret shows, turning email into a reward, not just an info stream.
  • Monthly Highlight Reel: Recap of top photos, fan quotes, and best live moments, making fans feel seen and celebrated.

Why it’s effective:

Email isn’t trendy - it’s timeless. While socials grab attention, email builds commitment. It's also owned data - if Instagram vanished tomorrow, we’d still have our direct fanbase.

6. Key Metrics

At 130dB, we focus on metrics that reflect real fan engagement, community building, and financial sustainability.

Our performance will be monitored across three primary areas: Digital Engagement, Fanbase Growth, and Revenue and Retention. These metrics were chosen based on our business goals, interview insights, and channel strategy.

1. Click-Through Rate (CTR) – Website and Social

Why it matters:
CTR tells us how effective our content is at converting interest into action. Whether it’s a “Buy Tickets” button on our website or a swipe-up link on Instagram, this metric reflects fan excitement and intent.

What we’re tracking:

  • Instagram > Website click-throughs (from stories, link-in-bio, and posts)
  • Email > Website click-throughs (from newsletters and announcements)
  • Bounce rate vs. time on site (to ensure users are staying and engaging)

Success looks like:

  • 3 - 5% CTR on Instagram stories (industry avg. ~1.5 - 3%) (Rival IQ, 2024)
  • 10% email CTR for show announcements (Chaffey, 2024)
  • Increasing page views for artist lineups, loyalty programs, and event pages

2. Newsletter Sign-Ups – Fanbase Growth

Why it matters:
Email subscribers are our most committed audience. They are the fans who opt in to hear from us regularly. This is owned data, and it becomes more valuable over time as we provide these fans with exclusive access and loyalty perks.

What we’re tracking:

  • Total sign-ups per month
  • Sign-up source (on-site QR, social, website, merch table, etc.)
  • Open rates and engagement over time

Success looks like:

  • 300 - 500 new sign-ups in the first 3 months post-launch
  • 35 - 50% average open rate (vs. industry avg. ~20–25%) (GetResponse, 2024)
  • Less than 1% unsubscribe rate (indicating high content satisfaction)

3. Ticket Sales

Why it matters:
Ticket sales are our main revenue stream, but they also tell us which shows generate buzz, how effective our marketing is, and which nights or artists attract loyalty-driven repeat attendance.

What we’re tracking:

  • Tickets sold per event (early bird vs. last-minute)
  • Revenue per event
  • Return attendees (tracked via loyalty accounts or repeat email opens)
  • Average revenue per attendee (including drinks/merch upsell)

Success looks like:

  • 70–90% average capacity on weekend shows
  • Sell-out for “New Blood Nights” within 6 months
  • 30%+ of attendees returning within 60 days
